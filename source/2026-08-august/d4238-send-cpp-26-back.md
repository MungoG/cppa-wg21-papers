## Abstract

Contracts entered C++26 through procedural moves that broke no rule and left no record that the sustained technical opposition to the design was ever reconciled.

WG21 decides by consensus, which ISO defines as the reconciliation of conflicting arguments rather than a count of votes. The Contracts proposal advanced on a roadmap that made forward motion the default, with the Technical Specification path closed, papers merged faster than many members could review them, and a long series of incremental polls whose accumulation was treated as a mandate no single vote had granted. When the design was carried over a sustained Strongly-Against minority, the record preserved the tally and the label "Consensus" but no finding that the opposition had been answered, and the papers that report the history do not always match the meeting record.

The same authors are now reusing and sharpening these moves on P3100, booking the committee's telecon calendar in advance and bundling direction, attendance, and pre-ratification into single questions. None of it broke a rule, which is why the working group cannot catch it from inside: a process built to produce consensus on time will produce it, whether or not the design beneath was audited. The one review ISO placed outside the working group is the National Body ballot, and the C++ Alliance's position is that the National Bodies return C++26 for the evaluation it skipped, at the cost of one cycle.

The author provides information and serves at the pleasure of the committee. This paper requests no floor time, no poll, and no committee action; it states a position and two recommendations, addressed to the National Bodies and the convener.

## 1. Introduction: The Alliance Position

This paper requests no floor time, no poll, and no committee action. It reports findings and states a position and two recommendations, both addressed to the National Bodies and the convener, not to the WG21 floor.

The C++ Alliance's position is that the National Bodies should vote No on the C++26 DIS ballot, with technical comments, as [ISO/IEC Directives Part 1, clause 2.6.2](https://www.iso.org/sites/directives/current/consolidated/index.html) provides, and return the draft for the technical evaluation the Contracts design did not receive. The Alliance further recommends that the convener state that the committee's officers will not penalize any delegate or National Body for casting that vote.

These are two independent grounds. A National Body persuaded only that the Contracts design is not ready - on the technical record alone, and setting aside any account of how it advanced - has sufficient basis to vote No. The procedural findings that follow explain how a design carrying sustained technical opposition reached the ballot without that opposition being reconciled; they strengthen the case but are not a precondition for it.

This paper contributes:

1. an account of the procedural moves by which Contracts entered C++26 (Section 2);
2. evidence that the same authors are reusing and sharpening those moves on P3100 (Section 3);
3. an analysis of why the record shows counting rather than the reconciliation ISO's definition of consensus describes (Section 4); and
4. the constitutional basis for the National Body ballot as the remaining external check (Section 5).

The paper assumes that the procedural moves it describes were compliant with SD-4 and the ISO Directives; it does not allege that any rule was broken. It further assumes that the poll tallies it cites are accurate as recorded.

## 2. What Happened Then: P2900

Every move in this section was available within SD-4 and the ISO Directives; none required a rule to be broken. The account below draws on the authors' own published rationale, [P2899](https://wg21.link/p2899), and on the recorded poll results.

**The Roadmap Ratchet.** In November 2022 SG21 adopted a roadmap for a Contracts MVP targeting C++26, recorded in [P2899](https://wg21.link/p2899) with the clause that the roadmap will "need consensus in SG21 to revise" (Kona, 2022-11-12: 10/10/5/1/1). Once that clause was in force, forward motion was the default, and any proposal to slow down was itself a revision of the roadmap, subject to the same consensus determination the chair controlled. Starting the schedule cost one poll; stopping it required assembling a second consensus against the people who had set the first. The direction of travel was fixed before the design was settled.

**The Closed TS Path.** A Technical Specification would have let implementations ship Contracts and gather field experience before the design entered the International Standard, and SG21 twice declined that path (SG21, 2022-12: 15 for, 3 against, 0 abstaining, to follow the roadmap rather than pursue a TS; SG21, 2024-05-30: 1/3/3/12/10 against a TS, a session at which the single pro-TS paper was outnumbered two to one by author papers scheduled against it). With the TS route closed, the only remaining path was the IS pipeline, where the C++26 deadline supplied the time pressure that later complaints described. Gabriel Dos Reis, who favored more field experience, wrote that "rushing the current P2900 into the IS is among the worse mistakes we could make, ranking way ahead of a TS that permits evaluation of the various conjectures in P2900" ([SG21 reflector, 2024-05-23](https://lists.isocpp.org/sg21/2024/05/8894.php)). John Spicer, who chaired those sessions, later concluded that "P2900 doesn't make anything better, and makes quite a lot worse" ([ext reflector, 2025-10-24](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2025/10/26208.php)).

**Contradictory Consensus.** The treatment of contracts on virtual functions was decided and reversed repeatedly: EWG merged the caller/callee model ([P3097](https://wg21.link/p3097)) into [P2900](https://wg21.link/p2900) (St. Louis EWG, 2024-06-28: 18/15/5/1/2) and then removed virtual functions again (Hagenberg, 2025-02: 20/24/13/14/2). Each determination carried the label "consensus," yet they pointed in opposite directions, so the word recorded only what the room decided on a given day, not a stable technical position. Bjarne Stroustrup, reviewing the surviving model, wrote that it "is novel, unique, untried, untested, and unimplemented. That alone should disqualify it from inclusion in an international standard" ([ext reflector, 2026-06-12](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2026/06/27121.php)).

**Compressed Review Window.** [P3443R0](https://wg21.link/p3443r0) recorded the tempo of the process: 63 papers handled in ten months, a majority of them merged into P2900 less than a week after publication. At that rate the only participants who could track each change were those working on Contracts full time, which left little review time for anyone else.

**The Consensus Ratchet.** P2900 advanced not through one decisive vote but through a long series of incremental polls, each merged change becoming the settled baseline the next poll built upon. [P2899](https://wg21.link/p2899) records dozens of such determinations, and the 63 papers in ten months counted by [P3443R0](https://wg21.link/p3443r0) fed that accumulation. No single poll ever asked the room to approve the design as a whole; the mandate was assembled one reversible-looking increment at a time, until the accumulation itself was treated as the mandate. The polls that did forward the design were themselves counts recorded as consensus over a standing minority: EWG sent P2900R11 to CWG for C++26 (Wroclaw, 2024-11-18: 25/17/0/3/12) and LEWG forwarded it the same week (Wroclaw, 2024-11-21: 23/9/1/0/5), each carrying a double-digit or five-vote Strongly-Against bloc and neither recording a finding that the opposition had been answered.

**The Reversed Burden.** Once the increments had accumulated, removing the feature required a fresh consensus the objectors could not assemble: the motion to remove P2900 from C++26 failed (Hagenberg, 2025-02: 9/8/3/19/41), so a divided room preserved the feature by default. Adding to the standard and removing from it both require consensus, and every accumulated approval shifted the burden further onto whoever wished to stop, independent of the technical merits.

**Rebuttal by Volume.** Objections to P2900 were met with counter-papers longer than the objections and authored by the same people who controlled scheduling: [P3327R0](https://wg21.link/p3327r0) answered function-pointer concerns, and the eighteen-page [P3591R0](https://wg21.link/p3591r0) (by Berne and Doumler) and the twenty-two-author [P3846R0](https://wg21.link/p3846r0) answered design and national-body concerns. Presented alongside the objection and then put to a vote, these papers let volume and authorship settle questions their substance did not. Ville Voutilainen described the pattern as "multiple other such rebuttal papers that end up being dismissal papers" ([ext reflector, 2025-02-03](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2025/02/24718.php)).

## 3. What Is Happening Now: P3100

The authors who ran the Contracts process are running P3100. Timur Doumler and Joshua Berne are the authors of P3100 ([P3100R6](https://wg21.link/p3100r6)) and were authors of the Contracts rationale [P2899R1](https://wg21.link/p2899r1) and of [P2900R14](https://wg21.link/p2900r14). The moves below are the same techniques described in Section 2, applied again and, in several cases, sharpened.

**Calendar Pre-Allocation.** Where the Contracts process applied pace as pressure in the move named Compressed Review Window, P3100 books the committee's calendar in advance: a standing series of EWG telecons was brought into being by poll to review the paper one item at a time (Sofia, 2025-06: 15/27/2/0/0 for per-clause approval in telecons). A review cadence voted into existence commits the room's time to one proposal for months, and competing designs cannot be scheduled into slots already spoken for. Voutilainen, objecting to a session convened before its materials existed, wrote that "the meeting's agenda has no paper to discuss" and that there was "no chance that any member of the audience has prepared for this meeting" ([ext reflector, 2026-07-11](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2026/07/27248.php)).

**The Consensus Ratchet, Returned.** This move sharpens the technique named The Consensus Ratchet in Section 2. P3100 is being advanced through roughly eighty individual undefined-behavior line items ([P3100R6](https://wg21.link/p3100r6), Appendix A), each reviewed and approved on its own so the accumulation can be presented as settled history. The one poll that asked the room to move the paper forward failed (Croydon, 2026-03-26: 1/17/10/18/14), and P3100's own published history reports only the same-day poll to continue case-by-case review (39/19/5/2/1), not the failed forward vote. The design advances by many small approvals while the single adverse result on the design as a whole is absent from the record the paper keeps of itself.

**The Reversed Burden, Pre-Assembled.** This move sharpens the technique named The Reversed Burden in Section 2. The line-item approvals are being accumulated ahead of the B&uacute;zios meeting, where the paper's forward motion will be put as one question against a stack of decisions already recorded as settled. As with Contracts, this arranges matters so that stopping the proposal, rather than advancing it, becomes the position that must assemble consensus. The burden is being shifted before the decisive vote is taken.

**Slide-as-Basis.** A single presentation slide (slide 53 of [P3754R0](https://wg21.link/p3754r0)) was polled into becoming the structural basis of the safety work product (Sofia, 2025-06: 14/28/2/1/0 that the slide is "a good basis" for the undefined-behavior white paper). Polling a slide into the role of foundation fixes an architectural choice through a process vote, before that choice is examined as a design on its own terms.

**The Compound Poll.** The Brno direction poll bundled three distinct questions into one: approval of the paper's direction, a commitment to attend the review telecons, and pre-authorization to reconsider the outcome at B&uacute;zios (Brno, 2026-06: 16/15/6/2/0). A member who supported one element had to endorse all three, and the count of those willing to attend was thereby converted into a claimed mandate for the direction.

**Option-Menu Control.** The set of options presented to the room has been curated so that alternatives threatening P3100 are absent from the menu. Voutilainen, whose non-throwing enforcement semantic was left off the choices, noted that when a design goes "selecting an option from a sub-menu within quick_enforce, it's then suggested to be doing the wrong thing" ([ext reflector, 2026-07-21](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2026/07/27431.php)), and pressed the evidentiary standard the process has avoided: "Bring me deployment experience. Then we have a basis for voting" ([ext reflector, 2026-07-17](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2026/07/27355.php)). A published alternative that never appears on the ballot cannot prevail: [P4297R0](https://wg21.link/p4297r0) exists, yet Profiles remains in SG23 while Contracts proceeds through EWG.

## 4. Counting, Not Reconciliation

ISO's definition of consensus is about reconciliation, not a count of hands, and WG21's own guidance says the same. At the subgroup level nothing required the determinations to meet that description, and they did not: they were called by the numbers, and the record does not preserve what would let anyone check them. That gap is not a broken rule.

**The Unreconciled Override.** ISO's definition of consensus is a process that seeks to take the views of all parties into account and to reconcile any conflicting arguments ([ISO/IEC Directives Part 1, clause 2.5.6](https://www.iso.org/sites/directives/current/consolidated/index.html), quoting ISO/IEC Guide 2); at the committee-draft stage it directs the leadership to register sustained opposition and to try in good faith to resolve it. At the subgroup level, where these determinations were made, ISO specifies no such procedure - [SD-4](https://isocpp.org/std/standing-documents/sd-4-wg21-practices-and-procedures) governs, and it leaves the determination of consensus to the chair. A subgroup chair who declared consensus over sustained opposition therefore broke no rule. What ISO's definition describes as consensus, though - reconciliation, not a headcount - is nowhere shown in the record: consensus was declared over sustained Strongly-Against blocs with only the tally recorded and no finding that the opposition was addressed. Recording that finding is best practice, not a subgroup requirement, which is why the gap cannot be corrected from inside the process. Vinnie Falco (the author of this paper) put it precisely: "The chair may have excellent reasons for determining consensus after a 3.7:1 poll, but those reasons aren't documented. The number stands alone as the artifact" ([ext reflector, 2026-06-12](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2026/06/27123.php)).

**Counting Over Reconciliation.** Consensus is reached by addressing objections, not by counting them: the IETF states the point directly for its own process, that consensus turns on whether objections are resolved rather than on percentages ([RFC 7282](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7282.html)), and WG21's own guidance says the same, "we make decisions by consensus, not majority" ([P2195R2](https://wg21.link/p2195r2)). In the Contracts and P3100 records, consensus was instead called from vote ratios over sustained Strongly-Against minorities. J. Daniel Garc&iacute;a stated the distinction plainly: "There is a difference between consensus and majority that should not be ignored" ([SG21 reflector, 2025-10-17](https://lists.isocpp.org/sg21/2025/10/11355.php)). Bjarne Stroustrup, reviewing the Brno straw poll, added that "5 strongly against is significant, and the opposition has been sustained" ([ext reflector, 2026-06-12](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2026/06/27121.php)).

**Poll Curation.** The record the authors publish does not always match the record the meeting kept. P3100's published revision history reports the Croydon same-day poll to continue case-by-case review (39/19/5/2/1) but omits the forward poll taken the same day, which failed (Croydon EWG, 2026-03-26: 1/17/10/18/14). In the Contracts record, a Tokyo poll that the committee's public poll record marked "not meant to be binding, but rather to offer a first set of feedback to the authors," with no result recorded (Tokyo EWG, 2024-03-20, Poll 8: 17/12/12/12/5, [cplusplus/papers #1648](https://github.com/cplusplus/papers/issues/1648#issuecomment-2009017161)), is reproduced in the authors' rationale paper without that qualification, beside polls that carry a recorded result such as "Result: Consensus against" ([P2899R1](https://wg21.link/p2899r1); the same tally is also printed in [P3197R0](https://wg21.link/p3197r0)). From the published papers alone a reader cannot tell which polls were binding determinations and which the committee had explicitly set aside as non-binding feedback.

## 5. The Remedy: The National Body Ballot

A determination that is never required to be recorded or published can be tested only by an audience outside the working group, and that audience is the National Bodies at the ballot. Checks and balances are what keep the power honest: when those who can override sustained opposition know their determination will be read and weighed at the ballot, the incentive to reconcile in good faith is preserved; remove that oversight and the incentive decays. The position and recommendations below restore the check.

**The Subtractive Last Line of Defense.** The National Body ballot is the only mechanism in the ISO system that can remove a feature after a working group has adopted it, and it sits outside the working group by design so that the people closest to the work are not the only ones who check it. A negative vote accompanied by technical comments is unconditionally valid, and approval requires both a two-thirds majority and no more than one quarter negative, so seven negatives among twenty-six voting P-members return the document ([ISO/IEC Directives Part 1, clauses 2.6.2, 2.6.3, 2.6.5](https://www.iso.org/sites/directives/current/consolidated/index.html); the C++26 CD ballot [N5028](https://wg21.link/n5028) recorded three National Bodies voting No). The power is real and recent: the trivial-relocation feature ([P2786](https://wg21.link/p2786)) was removed from C++26 after National Bodies objected, and [P4043R0](https://wg21.link/p4043r0) records more than twenty National Body comments on Contracts, several seeking its removal or deferral at the Committee Draft stage. J. Daniel Garc&iacute;a has warned that "there is a serious risk that for C++26 we get NB voting abstention or even against the IS," which "would be the first time for ISO 14882" ([ext reflector, 2026-03-08](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2026/03/26973.php)).

The return threshold is not the only reason to cast the vote. Every C++ DIS ballot from C++11 through C++23 passed unanimously; the C++26 CD ballot already broke that pattern, with three National Bodies voting No ([N5028](https://wg21.link/n5028)). A negative vote with technical comments, even short of the one-quarter that returns the draft, is a determination the committee cannot set aside: [clause 2.6.5](https://www.iso.org/sites/directives/current/consolidated/index.html) directs that "every attempt shall be made to resolve negative votes," which places the objection and the committee's answer on the permanent record and lowers the cost of the same scrutiny in later cycles. The technical comments such a vote carries stand on their own: the novelty and lack of implementation Stroustrup describes, and the absence of deployment experience that Finland's Committee Draft comment and Voutilainen both pressed, are ballot substance independent of any procedural account. And returning the draft targets one contested feature at the cost of a single cycle; it does not reject reflection, execution, or the rest of C++26, which continue on their published paths.

The comment stage is not a substitute for the vote. The formal disposition of comments and its per-comment tracker are not public, and the Contracts comments that were engaged at all were dispositioned by poll rather than by a recorded reconciliation: the response paper to Romania's comment RO 2-056 ([P4044R0](https://wg21.link/p4044r0)) failed (Croydon EWG, 2026-03-26, poll "Forward P4044R0 to CWG for inclusion in C++26 as a response to RO 2-056": 3/4/0/22/23, not consensus). Gabriel Dos Reis, responding to a proposal that every National Body comment receive a default reply of no change, asked "Is the suggestion here that NB should just rubber stamp? If so, what is the point of the ballot? Why not go directly to publication?" ([ext reflector, 2025-08-14](https://lists.isocpp.org/ext/2025/08/26008.php)). Because the comment stage resolves this way, the ballot vote is the check that remains, and the C++ Alliance's position is that the National Bodies should vote No on the C++26 DIS, with technical comments, so that the design receives the evaluation it has not yet had.

**Removing the Penalty for Voting No.** A right that carries a social cost ensures that the time it is most needed, will be the time it is least used. [SD-4](https://isocpp.org/std/standing-documents/sd-4-wg21-practices-and-procedures) states that a member who regularly uses the escalation process "erodes their credibility," a penalty the Directives nowhere attach to an unconditional ballot right ([ISO/IEC Directives Part 1, clauses 2.6.2, 5.1.2](https://www.iso.org/sites/directives/current/consolidated/index.html)), and the informal treatment of a No vote as disloyalty deters National Bodies from casting one. The convener has the standing to remove that deterrent. The C++ Alliance recommends that the convener state that the committee's officers will not penalize a delegate or a National Body for voting No, so that the last line of defense is available in fact and not only on paper. This is offered as a course the committee may adopt, not a demand, and it requests no floor time.

## 6. Conclusion

The set of procedural moves that carried Contracts into C++26 is now carrying P3100, and every one of them was permitted by the rules. That is the difficulty. A process followed exactly can still deliver a feature the record cannot show was reconciled with its sustained opposition, and the working group cannot detect this from the inside, because nothing inside the working group requires the reconciliation to be written down. The National Body ballot is the one review ISO placed outside the working group for exactly this case.

The C++ Alliance's position is that the National Bodies return C++26 for the technical evaluation it has not yet received, at the cost of a single cycle; this returns one contested feature, not reflection, execution, or the rest of the standard, and the work continues on published paths in the meantime. The Alliance further recommends that the convener state plainly that no officer will penalize a delegate or a National Body for a No vote, so that the check the system depends on is available in practice. C++ gains a feature that has been evaluated before it is locked into the standard and the ABI it ships; it keeps paying, if the draft is ratified unexamined, for a defect that consensus procedure alone could not surface.
