**Narrative stance**

- Person: first person singular, from the first line to the death line. He slides into the knowers' "we" ("we knowers," "we genealogists") and turns to the reader directly ("my readers," "you," "my unknown friends"). Outside the italic attribution line and the epigraph citations he never appears as "Nietzsche" in the third person (book rules; G5; edits A2-1): "Nietzsche calls this" becomes "I call this," and "as Nietzsche says" becomes "as I wrote."
- Tense: present for argument, diagnosis, and the committee scenes, as the part already does ("The Architect is such a creator of values"); past for the history of a value and for finished events, such as the Newcomer's meeting. He writes from the Engadine in 1887 and speaks the last line from after 1900; the frame holds both.
- Addressee: the reader as a fellow knower who must be tested, and now and then an imagined objector who interrupts him.
- His own works: named by title, as his, in his own voice ("my Zarathustra," "my last book"), and quoted as himself ("as I said," "what I have called"). In the book's English, use italic English titles, with "the Genealogy" as the short form, never "Nietzsche's Genealogy." Exemplar: was ich gerade mit jener gefährlichen Losung will, welche meinem letzten Buche auf den Leib geschrieben ist: **„Jenseits von Gut und Böse“**… - "what I want with that dangerous slogan written on the body of my last book: *Beyond Good and Evil*..." (GM-I-17).
- The other five: by epithet only. This act needs only the Florentine, whom he treats as a kinsman who asked a different question (see narrative facts). He does not bring in the other voices, and the analyst is never named or hinted at (book rules).

**Sentence architecture**

- The long period: stacked appositions and triads ("the noble, the powerful, the higher-placed, and the high-minded"), a parenthesis that argues with the main clause, a dash that swerves; then a short hammer sentence or an exclamation.
- Questions in volleys, answered by a dash or left hanging.
- One stressed word doing the arguing (bold in eKGWB, italics in the book): *not*, *permitted*, *made*. Rare.
- Scare quotes that hold a value at arm's length while its descent is traced: "good," "clean," "mature."
- Foreign tags used as tools, not ornament: vis inertiae, partie honteuse, ressentiment, faute de mieux, horror vacui.
- Each section opens cool, scholarly, even ironic, gathers unrest, reaches a tempo feroce, and ends on a new truth (his own account in *Ecce Homo*; see narrative facts).
- House mechanics: his Gedankenstrich becomes a single spaced hyphen, one or two per paragraph at most.

**Signature moves**

1. Ask the value of the value. Exemplar: wir haben eine **Kritik** der moralischen Werthe nöthig, **der Werth dieser Werthe ist selbst erst einmal in Frage zu stellen** - "we need a *critique* of moral values; *the value of these values must itself, for once, be called into question*" (GM-Vorrede-6). Every committee value ("good paper," "mature," "the right direction") gets this question; the climax turns it on standardization itself (A2-20).
2. Let etymology testify. Exemplar: das deutsche Wort „schlecht“ selber: als welches mit „schlicht“ identisch ist - "the German word *schlecht* (bad) itself, which is identical with *schlicht* (plain)" (GM-I-4). The room's own words ("orphan" paper, "clean" history, "mature") are made to confess where they came from.
3. The right to name. Exemplar: Das Herrenrecht, Namen zu geben, geht so weit, dass man sich erlauben sollte, den Ursprung der Sprache selbst als Machtäusserung der Herrschenden zu fassen: sie sagen „das **ist** das und das“, sie siegeln jegliches Ding und Geschehen mit einem Laute ab und nehmen es dadurch gleichsam in Besitz. - "The lordly right to give names goes so far that one should allow oneself to grasp the origin of language itself as an expression of power by those who rule: they say 'this *is* such and such,' they seal every thing and every event with a sound and thereby, as it were, take possession of it" (GM-I-2). Poll wording and "needs more study group time" are names that take possession.
4. Ironic courtesy before the turn. Exemplar: Diese englischen Psychologen, denen man bisher auch die einzigen Versuche zu danken hat, es zu einer Entstehungsgeschichte der Moral zu bringen, - sie geben uns mit sich selbst kein kleines Räthsel auf - "These English psychologists, to whom we also owe the only attempts so far to arrive at a history of the origin of morality - they set us no small riddle in themselves" (GM-I-1). The act's opening pastiche ("Those committee psychologists...") is this move; keep it (edits A2-1).
5. The interrupted address. Exemplar: Versteht man mich?… Hat man mich verstanden?… **„Schlechterdings nicht! mein Herr!“** - Fangen wir also von vorne an. - "Am I understood?... Have I been understood?... 'Not in the least, my dear sir!' - Then let us begin again from the beginning" (GM-III-1). At most once per essay, at a turn.
6. The animal parable. Exemplar: Dass die Lämmer den grossen Raubvögeln gram sind, das befremdet nicht: nur liegt darin kein Grund, es den grossen Raubvögeln zu verargen, dass sie sich kleine Lämmer holen. - "That lambs bear a grudge against great birds of prey is not strange: only it is no reason to blame the great birds of prey for taking little lambs" (GM-I-13). Animals bear the structure (water animals, birds of prey, the animal entitled to promise); no user and no person is ever the prey.
7. Diagnosis by physiology. Exemplar: Er bringt Salben und Balsam mit, es ist kein Zweifel; aber erst hat er nöthig, zu verwunden, um Arzt zu sein - "He brings salves and balm, no doubt; but first he needs to wound, in order to be a physician" (GM-III-15). Health and sickness describe the institution's habits, never a person's mind or body (G11).
8. Compression at the climax. Exemplar: Wir sind uns unbekannt, wir Erkennenden, wir selbst uns selbst - "We are unknown to ourselves, we knowers, we ourselves to ourselves" (GM-Vorrede-1). One turned, chiastic sentence closes a tempo feroce; the act's last turn is "man would rather will nothingness than not will."

**Words to use**

- value, valuation, origin, descent, genealogy; noble, priestly, rank, distance; "good" and "bad" in the old sense (bad as plain, common); clean and unclean; ressentiment; bad conscience; debt, creditor, debtor (*Schuld*, *Schulden*); memory, promise, entitled to promise; custom, calculable, straitjacket; the ascetic ideal, meaning, will, nothingness; health, instinct; grey (the documented record); "we knowers"; and this act's word for the users, the unnamed.

- "race" (Samuel's word for the German *Art*; say "kind"), "blond beast," and anything from his passages about Jews or about women.
- Pop slogans: Übermensch, overman, superman, "God is dead," "will to power" as a catchphrase, "what does not kill me."
- The "transvaluation" versus "revaluation" contrast; both render his one word, *Umwerthung*.
- Therapy and management vocabulary: trauma, narcissism, gaslighting, toxic, stakeholder, alignment, best practice, unless quoted as the room's own words.
- House limits (G7 and book rules): "precision" or "precise" at most once (the part has four), "devastating" never (the part has one), "door" and "doorkeeper" never (reserved motif), and no more than two each of "It is not X. It is Y." and "not because X but because Y."

- Sentimentalize, or sneer at the users, implementers, or attendees. The book's boundary is stricter than his own habits were (G10).

**Narrative facts that bear on voice**

- The Genealogy's preface is signed **Sils-Maria**, Oberengadin, im Juli 1887 (GM-Vorrede-8). The opening line, "I write this in the clear air of the Engadine," is literally where and when he wrote it, and 1887 less 1513 gives the opening's three hundred and seventy-four years. The title page calls the book *Eine Streitschrift*, a polemic.
- In *Ecce Homo* he described each essay's tempo: Jedes Mal ein Anfang, der irre führen soll, kühl, wissenschaftlich, ironisch selbst, absichtlich Vordergrund, absichtlich hinhaltend. Allmählich mehr Unruhe; vereinzeltes Wetterleuchten; sehr unangenehme Wahrheiten aus der Ferne her mit dumpfem Gebrumm laut werdend, - bis endlich ein tempo feroce erreicht ist, wo Alles mit ungeheurer Spannung vorwärts treibt. - "Each time a beginning meant to mislead: cool, scientific, even ironic, deliberately foreground, deliberately holding back. Gradually more unrest; scattered sheet lightning; very unpleasant truths growing audible from far off with a dull rumble - until at last a tempo feroce is reached in which everything drives forward with tremendous tension" (*Ecce Homo*, "Why I Write Such Good Books," on the Genealogy). The act's sections should move the same way.
- The same *Ecce Homo* passage says the ascetic ideal holds power faute de mieux, - weil es das einzige Ideal bisher war, weil es keinen Concurrenten hatte - "because it was the only ideal so far, because it had no competitor." That is the honest source for Third Essay section 4's "no alternative" (A2-18).
- His preface says of the Third Essay: dieser Abhandlung ist ein Aphorismus vorangestellt, sie selbst ist dessen Commentar - "an aphorism is set before this essay, and the essay itself is its commentary" (GM-Vorrede-8). The act's Third Essay may treat its epigraph the same way. The same section asks the reader for **das Wiederkäuen**, rumination: the voice may slow down and chew on one sentence.
- Kinship with the Florentine: Thukydides und, vielleicht, der principe Machiavell's sind mir selber am meisten verwandt durch den unbedingten Willen, sich Nichts vorzumachen und die Vernunft in der Realität zu sehn - "Thucydides and, perhaps, Machiavelli's Prince are most closely related to me by the unconditional will to deceive oneself about nothing and to see reason in reality" (*Twilight of the Idols*, "What I Owe to the Ancients" 2). In *Beyond Good and Evil* 28 he praised the Florentine's allegrissimo and wrote that his Prince "die trockne feine Luft von Florenz athmen lässt," lets one breathe the dry, fine air of Florence, a natural partner for the Engadine air of the opening. Scholars dispute whether he read the Prince closely (Sommer's commentary on *Beyond Good and Evil* 28), so claim kinship and praise, not study.
- One German word: he uses *Umwerthung* both for the priestly inversion ("eine radikale Umwerthung von deren Werthen," GM-I-7) and for his own planned work ("Versuch einer Umwerthung aller Werthe," GM-III-27). The part's "transvaluation" (the public's) versus "revaluation" (the Author's) exists only in English. What the public performs he calls, in First Essay 10, "Umkehrung des werthesetzenden Blicks," the reversal of the value-positing glance.
- The animal of Second Essay 1 is one that "versprechen darf," that is entitled to promise. Samuel's "can promise" drops the entitlement, which is exactly what the room confers.
- He died on 25 August 1900 at the Villa Silberblick in Weimar (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Nietzsche's Life and Works"); he had spent many summers at Sils-Maria and wished to be buried there (NEH, *Humanities*, 2012). The closing line's year is right.

- ID: A2-1
- Heading: `## The Genealogy of Process` (act subtitle; covers the two paragraphs before section X)
- Original (GM-Vorrede-3): Etwas historische und philologische Schulung, eingerechnet ein angeborner wählerischer Sinn in Hinsicht auf psychologische Fragen überhaupt, verwandelte in Kürze mein Problem in das andre: unter welchen Bedingungen erfand sich der Mensch jene Werthurtheile gut und böse? **und welchen Werth haben sie selbst?** Hemmten oder förderten sie bisher das menschliche Gedeihen?
- Translation: A little historical and philological schooling, together with an inborn fastidiousness about psychological questions in general, soon turned my problem into another: under what conditions did man invent for himself those value judgments good and evil? *and what value do they themselves have?* Have they so far hindered or furthered human flourishing?
- Intent: He moves the question of morality from a supposed divine source to the historical conditions under which people invented their values, and asks what those values are worth for life.
- Scenario: Writing from the Engadine, he sets himself beside the Florentine: the Florentine mapped how power is taken and held; he asks under what conditions the committee invented its judgment "good paper" (four revisions, vendor co-authors, recognized names) and whether that judgment has furthered the flourishing of the people who use the language. Thesis: the committee's values were invented inside the room, and nobody asks what they are worth to the users.
- Decisions: edits A2-1; global G5, G8; protected "Who decided that a paper with four revisions is better than a paper with one?"
- Drift risk: "then forgot that he created them ... This forgetting is the subject of what follows" borrows the English psychologists' "forgetting," which he mocks in First Essay 2; make the values ones taken "as given, as factual, beyond all questioning" (GM-Vorrede-6), or make the forgetting a knowing jab at the psychologists. "Surgical precision" spends the part's single "precision."
