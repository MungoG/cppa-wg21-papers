# Chapter 9 Source Brief - What Goes Into a Proposal

- Purpose: condensed fact brief for the writer. Not book prose. Neutral paper-writing facts only.
- Sources: `wg21-bible` Part 4 and Part 6, plus `wg21-reform-codex.md` Section 2 only (Sections 1 and 5-9 excluded by task).
- Style note: the book bans "ecosystem" (sources say "ecosystem delivery") and Bible coined terms. Use "the wider C++ world" or "packages."

## The Floor vs the Bar

The hard requirements are procedural and low: a paper, in before the pre-meeting mailing deadline, with a prepared presenter in the room. No paper, no proposal. A late paper or an absent presenter is not actionable during meeting time. SD-4 sets the procedure. SD-7 governs paper numbers, submission, and format.

There is no ISO requirement that a feature be implemented before standardization (SD-4 states this plainly). So the evidentiary bar is the author's to supply, not the committee's. The floor is easy. The bar that makes a good standard is high and unenforced, and that gap is the chapter's subject.

## What Belongs in a Paper

Front matter carries title, document number, date, audience, reply-to, and intent. Every paper declares one of two intents:

- `intent: ask` proposes, requests a poll, seeks adoption. Opens with "This paper asks..."
- `intent: info` places evidence in the record and asks for nothing. Closes with "This paper asks for nothing."

Load-bearing parts, in order:

- Disclosure first. State what you maintain, believe, and cannot do, in specific terms ("the author maintains Library X," not "relevant experience"). Name one genuine limitation without a "however," since readers calibrate trust in the first two paragraphs.
- Title, abstract, conclusion each stand alone, since a busy delegate reads one and decides whether the body earns the rest. Write the conclusion first, then make the abstract promise only what it delivers.
- Body stacks evidence simplest to most complex, each step adding one dimension, so a reader who accepts step N cannot escape step N+1.
- Acknowledgments trace every contribution to its author.

Four arguments a proposal carries, each below: motivation (evidence of need), design, alternatives considered, implementation experience.

## The Abstract as Elevator Pitch

It must read to a competent C++ programmer who was not in the room. The narrow-lens review test is exactly that: "understand it from the paper alone." First sentence is a standalone fact, not a wind-up. The title names the finding, not the topic. Not marketing: promise only what the body delivers. Reform Codex 2.11 wants one bold one-liner per major section.

## Tony Tables / Before-After Comparison

The before/after table is the argument, not a summary of it. Show two designs side by side, label which is which, and delete the adjective, because the reader can see which is shorter. An empty cell is itself a finding. Include the libraries that contradict your position, which makes the table unimpeachable.

## The Steel Man Requirement

Two steel men, each stated in full and then defeated with evidence:

1. Against standardization. The strongest case that the wider C++ world is enough without a standard feature.
2. Of competing designs, including doing nothing. The strongest case for each alternative, then why this design wins.

Craft rule (Reform Codex 2.5): honor opposing designs first, naming three specific achievements of a rival before its costs. Critique designs, not designers.

## Implementation Experience

Why required: a standard feature is frozen at adoption, implemented by every vendor, taught in every curriculum, ABI-stable for decades. The room wants proof before the freeze.

What counts:

- Library: code that compiles, passes tests, carries benchmarks, and documents its API.
- Language feature: a proof-of-concept compiler fork showing the feature, its edge cases, and its interaction with existing features.
- The quality bar is deliberately low. Not production-grade. It must compile, pass tests, and demonstrate the design.

How much: Reform Codex 2.2 asks for at least two independent, tested implementations before adoption. "Implementation experience: none" now reads as a choice to arrive unarmed, since a complete tested library is days of work with AI assistance. When even AI cannot scaffold a language-feature proof of concept, that difficulty is itself evidence of unevaluated cost.

How to get it: build the library, ship it to real independent users, fix failure modes in the open, for years before proposing. Five rungs of independent adoption: authors built it, independent developers used it, independent libraries built on it, independent applications on those, real users in production. Model: `{fmt}` shipped on GitHub in 2012, earned tens of thousands of users over eight years, and only then became `std::format`.

## The A-G Evidence Checklist (the bar the room applies)

Seven categories a reviewer runs against the paper (Part 6). It is distinct from the Landscape Survey, the artifact the author brings, where the checklist is the bar applied to it.

- A. Ecosystem Friction. Incompatible implementations in active use and the measurable cost of incompatibility (boundary conversion code, rolled-own, ad-hoc representations).
- B. Vocabulary Type Value. Two or more independent libraries that would put the type in their public interfaces. Inside one codebase it is a utility, not a vocabulary type.
- C. Language Blessing. What the type gains from the compiler that no portable library can: intrinsics, constexpr, structural-type status, diagnostics. Strongest single argument when real.
- D. Implementation Maturity. Production-tested implementations, stable algorithms, a reference implementation that builds and passes tests. Strategy-level maturity (allocation, ABI, exceptions) is rarer and matters more.
- E. Cost Assessment. Implementer estimates, spec page count against comparable additions, interaction map, committee and vendor time displaced.
- F. Comparative Analysis. Why the leading marketplace library cannot serve, why a vcpkg/Conan dependency will not, whether the type crosses boundaries in languages that ship it, why prior attempts died.
- G. Scope and Complexity Risk. Reviewable and votable as one coherent unit? Do deferred pieces corner the design? Usage outside the proposers?

A "yes" across all seven is rare. Most proposals fail B, fail the higher rungs of D, and never seriously attempt F.

## Motivation: "Useful Is Not Justification"

"We find this useful" is not a justification. The bar: "this cannot be delivered by the wider C++ world because X, and standardization provides Y that the wider world cannot." Skepticism scales with the claim: a narrow claim earns narrow scrutiny, a universal claim universal scrutiny. Properties only the standard supplies: portability, guaranteed availability, vocabulary-type coordination across libraries that do not know each other, ABI stability, compiler blessings.

## The Twelve Loop-Closing Elements + Falsification

The committee will not ask for these. Supply them anyway (Part 4 and Reform Codex 2.4):

1. Evidence of Need
2. Complete Implementation
3. Steel Man Against Standardization
4. Steel Man of Competing Designs
5. Post-Adoption Metrics (success criteria set before adoption)
6. Forced Retrospective (look-back at a fixed interval, fires whether or not anyone volunteers)
7. Decision Record (why this design, what was traded, when to revisit)
8. Domain Coverage Attestation (which domains were represented at the poll)
9. Prediction Registry (claims with falsifiable criteria and a revisit date)
10. Symmetric Evidence Bar (same standard for incumbent and challenger)
11. Knowledge Continuity (rationale that survives author turnover)
12. Outcome Tracking (did it ship, did implementers ship, did users adopt)

Falsification (Reform Codex 2.3): name the deployment result that would retract your proposal, or the paper is unfalsifiable by design.

Proportional rule: the artifact set scales with consequence. A routine, reversible change needs the paper plus an implementation. The full twelve are for hard-to-reverse decisions.

## Scope Control and Paper Splitting

One topic per paper. Split on the word "and" (Reform Codex 2.7). Staging is the default: break a large proposal into independently valuable stages, ship the useful primitives first, measure adoption, let evidence decide whether the rest is still needed. Staged approaches ship (Ranges, format, Coroutines, Concepts on the fourth attempt). Monolithic approaches stall (Networking TS, unified Executors, four big-integer attempts). A monolithic paper carries the burden: steel-man decomposition, then defeat it with evidence.

## Paper Dependencies

When a proposal depends on primitives shipping first, the decomposed-pipeline order is the dependency: ship the primitive, measure, build the next stage on it.

## Library Bar vs Language Bar (Core Language Is Hard Mode)

The library bar justifies a proposal against the wider C++ world: why in the standard rather than a library on GitHub, answered with evidence of need, a complete implementation, and a landscape survey. The language bar differs in kind, since the wider world cannot deliver a language feature at all, so "why not a library" does not apply. The question is whether the benefit justifies permanent complexity: every compiler, every curriculum, and every reader of code must absorb it. Artifacts become real-world before/after code, a proof-of-concept compiler fork, a teaching story, and the SD-10 design principles (compatibility, zero-overhead, safe-by-default, no viral or heavy annotation, intent not mechanism). A core proposal clears both, the harder path.

- FLAG: "discouraged until you've attended meetings" is not in the sources. The greater difficulty of language proposals is supported, the attend-first advice is not (confidence: high).

## The Cultural Norm: High Burden of Proof

The burden of proof is on the proposer. The default is do not standardize. Irreversibility is the reason: a shipped feature cannot be removed, and the ABI locks mistakes in place. `std::regex` has been slower than its alternatives for over a decade and cannot be repaired without breaking binaries. `std::unordered_map`'s mandated node structure leaves `boost::unordered_flat_map` roughly three times faster on string keys. Most papers do not make it: four big-integer attempts died over twenty-two years (author attrition, missing reference implementations, scope creep, allocator dilemma), and networking has been proposed since 2005 with nothing shipped in twenty-one years.

- FLAG: "every proposal starts at -100 points" is not in the sources. The norm (high burden, default no) is well supported. Attribute the quote from its own origin (confidence: high).

## Write for the 2032 Archive Reader

A paper has three readers: the room that votes, the chair that schedules, and the delegate who opens it cold years later. Write for the last one: expand acronyms, date every meeting, use retrievable sources (Reform Codex 2.15). The no-ask posture follows: an inform-paper that asks for nothing imposes no burden, so a chair schedules it, while a paper that asks for floor time competes for a scarce resource.
