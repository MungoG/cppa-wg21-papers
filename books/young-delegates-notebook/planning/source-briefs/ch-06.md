## Preparing before you go

Read in proportion to where you vote, not cover to cover. The committee publishes 300 to 500 papers a year across pre-meeting and post-meeting mailings, far more than anyone reads in full.

- **Find the mailing two ways.** The official archive at open-std.org is a flat chronological list of PDFs with no filter, search, or grouping. The enhanced mailing at wg21.org shows the same papers filtered by subgroup, full-text searchable, and grouped into per-room reports that link back to the open-std.org originals.
- **Filter to your subgroup(s) and read that room's agenda papers before you walk in.** You cannot follow six or seven rooms at once, so choose one and prepare for it.
- **Read the latest revision.** Paper numbers carry a revision suffix (P1234R0, R1, R2); read the highest. A `P` number is a proposal, an `N` number is administrative (agenda, minutes), and a `D` prefix is a pre-publication draft that does not resolve publicly. Every published number resolves at wg21.link (pattern `https://wg21.link/p1234r0`).
- **Mind the deadline.** Papers must arrive before the pre-meeting mailing deadline; late papers are not actionable and are not discussed in meeting time.
- **Read SD-4** (WG21 Practices and Procedures) for the decision model: the five-point straw-poll scale (Strongly Favor, Weakly Favor, Neutral, Weakly Against, Strongly Against) and consensus defined as "the absence of sustained opposition to substantial issues by any important part of the concerned interests."
- **The wiki.** SD-4 classifies meeting records, wikis, and reflector posts as protected materials that may not be quoted publicly. The sources confirm a WG21 wiki exists and is access-restricted, but give no URL and no "check the wiki" prep procedure. `(wiki-as-prep step NOT IN SOURCES)`

## Meeting frequency, locations, and rhythm

A meeting is week-long and runs six or seven parallel tracks (subgroup rooms) that converge on a closing plenary.

- **Cadence of the standard:** a new International Standard ships every three years (C++11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26, 29); P1000 codifies the cadence. This is the release train, not the meeting frequency.
- **Recent meetings (from the convener's trip reports):**
  - Brno, June 2026: ~200 attendees, 55% in person, 28 nations, 25 new guests, 22 subgroups, 6 tracks.
  - Sofia, June 2025: ~200 attendees, ~67% in person, 30 nations, 25 new guests, 23 subgroups, 7 tracks.
  - Source summary line: "Roughly 200 people, about 30 nations, two dozen new guests per meeting, hybrid onsite and online."
- **Overall scale:** roughly 350 active delegates from 24 to 31 nations.
- **FLAG:** the outline's "three meetings per year," a fixed location rotation, and "planning ahead" for venues are NOT stated in the sources. Both example meetings fall in June, in different cities (Brno, Sofia), which is consistent with rotation but does not establish a three-per-year schedule or any documented rotation pattern. `(NOT IN SOURCES - confirm from isocpp.org meetings page)`

## The shape of the week

- **Sunday 6pm orientation** for newcomers. The sources tie this to guests and new attendees and say it "sets the week's logistics and norms." Attend it if you are new.
- **The week:** parallel subgroup sessions (discussion, straw polls, revisions) that converge on the closing plenary, where adopted wording enters the working draft.
- **Objection deadline:** raise any "cannot live with" objection no later than 5pm the evening before closing plenary. SD-4 gives no weight in plenary to objections not escalated by then.
- **Minutes:** every session, telecons included, must be minuted, and any participant may be asked to take minutes.
- **FLAG:** the outline's "Mon-Sat six-day week," "8:30 AM to 5:30 PM," and "optional evening sessions" are NOT in the sources. The only time anchors the sources give are the Sunday 6pm orientation and the 5pm pre-plenary objection deadline. `(daily hours and six-day span NOT IN SOURCES)`

## Funding and cost

- **No registration fee appears anywhere in the sources.** Guest attendance is free and the Boost alternate path is free; only national-body membership carries a cost. ("No registration fee" is consistent with the sources but not stated verbatim - medium confidence inference, because two of three access paths are explicitly free.)
- **The three access paths and their cost:**
  - **National body member** (e.g. the US body, INCITS PL22.16): cost varies by nation, and the US body charges an annual fee. Membership is what lets you vote in plenary once your name is in the ISO LiveLink global directory.
  - **Boost Foundation alternate representative:** free, sponsored for C++ developers who cannot afford membership dues, and access is equivalent.
  - **Guest:** free. Verbatim: "Give the convener one week's notice, attend the Sunday 6pm orientation, and you take full part in subgroup work. Guests do everything except vote in plenary polls."
- **One week away from work:** meetings run a full week, so attending costs roughly a week of your time. (Supported indirectly via "week-long meetings.")

The only sponsorship described is the Boost Foundation covering membership dues, not travel, lodging, or meals. Travel, lodging, and meal costs are not discussed at all. `(travel funding and cost breakdown NOT IN SOURCES)`

## Your first meeting will be overwhelming, and that is normal

The sources support this beat strongly without needing embellishment.

- **You cannot cover everything.** Six or seven tracks run at once and you can be in one room at a time, so "full coverage is impossible." The named newcomer mistake is "trying to track every room and absorbing none."
- **The volume is real.** 300 to 500 papers a year, and the median delegate has an informed opinion on only about one in ten plenary polls.
- **You are not the only new person.** About two dozen (25) new guests attend each meeting, and a Sunday orientation exists specifically for them.
- **A coping move straight from the sources:** if your national body sends more than one delegate, divide the rooms and trade notes at day's end.
