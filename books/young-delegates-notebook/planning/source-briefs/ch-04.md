## The Reflector (committee mailing lists)

- The reflectors are subscription-restricted committee mailing lists.
- SD-4 designates meeting records, wikis, and reflector posts as "protected materials." You may not quote them publicly in papers or blog posts.
- Two exceptions to the no-quote rule:
  - Poll questions and numeric results may be quoted freely, with no permission.
  - A specific person's words or position may be quoted only with that person's prior consent.
- Everything else may only be paraphrased.
- In practice the consent exception is almost never used. Granting consent puts your words on the permanent public record, so almost no one asks and almost no one grants. It exists in the text, not in the practice.
- Behavior norms tied to sessions: the meeting guidelines bar live tweeting, blogging, photos, and video from sessions. Recording is not permitted. Source document is N4916.
- NOT in sources, flag: reflector subscription mechanics (the brief's "chair-gated, granted after your first meeting"), per-subgroup list names, and any tone or posting-etiquette norms beyond the protected-materials rule.

## Virtual vs in-person (how hybrid works)

- Meetings are hybrid, onsite and online.
- A meeting is one week of parallel subgroup sessions that converge on a closing plenary. Six or seven tracks run at once.
- You can only be in one room at a time. Covering six or seven tracks alone is impossible. Pick one room and prepare for it. If your national body sends more than one delegate, split the rooms and trade notes daily.
- Recent meeting shape, from the convener's trip reports:
  - Brno, June 2026: 200 attendees, 55% in person, 28 nations, 25 new guests, 22 subgroups, 6 tracks.
  - Sofia, June 2025: about 200 attendees, about 67% in person, 30 nations, 25 new guests, 23 subgroups, 7 tracks.
- What a remote attendee misses: the source states that pre-meeting hallway work, not the in-room argument, sets most outcomes, and that treating the hallway as mere social time is a mistake. Remote attendees cannot join that hallway track. (This is the only "what you miss" fact the sources support.)
- New attendees: there is a Sunday 6pm orientation. Guests give the convener one week's notice to attend.
- NOT in sources, flag: time zones and joining from the wrong continent. No facts on time-zone handling, session-hour fairness, or how online attendees are admitted to the virtual rooms.

## Following along remotely (mailings, trip reports, minutes)

- The committee publishes 300 to 500 papers a year across pre-meeting and post-meeting mailings. Read in proportion to where you vote.
- Trip reports: the convener, Herb Sutter, publishes public trip reports on his blog (herbsutter.com). The Brno and Sofia reports cited above are the examples. These summarize meeting outcomes for anyone, including non-attendees.
  - Brno trip report: https://herbsutter.com/2026/06/13/brno-trip-report/
  - Sofia trip report: https://herbsutter.com/2025/06/21/trip-report-june-2025-iso-c-standards-meeting-sofia-bulgaria/
- Minutes: every session, telecons included, must be minuted, and any participant may be asked to take minutes. Minutes and agendas are administrative documents that carry an N number (see paper numbering below).
- NOT in sources, flag: a dedicated public "meeting results" page, where minutes are posted for remote readers, and any straw-poll results page.

## wg21.org (the enhanced mailing and discussion platform)

- wg21.org is live infrastructure, volunteer-built and owned by the C++ Alliance. It is not an official committee site. It derives from public data and runs whether or not the committee cooperates.
- What it hosts:
  - The enhanced mailing, described as "the front door." You can browse the mailing filtered by subgroup (SG, EWG, LEWG, CWG, LWG, plenary), search full text, jump between pre- and post-meeting mailings, pull per-room reports, and batch-download a room's papers.
  - The papers themselves stay on open-std.org. wg21.org links to the originals.
  - The book *The Room*, in PDF and HTML.
  - Public mailing lists where new technology and AI tooling get discussed, including the paper-review list. The lists are readable without an account and are indexed by search engines.
- Paper review on the platform (a concrete way to take part remotely with no paper of your own):
  - Register with GitHub or Google. Your email stays hidden.
  - Read a scheduled paper from the public review list and post what you found. It need not be exhaustive.
  - The review window is exactly one week, Monday to Sunday.
  - An AI reviewer posts a narrow-lens analysis before the human window; the system attaches a credited summary PDF to the paper afterward.
  - Reviewing earns the standing to request a review of your own paper. Giving precedes asking.

## open-std.org (the official document repository)

- The official archive. A flat chronological list of PDFs, with no filter, no search, and no grouping.
- Papers are submitted to open-std.org mailings. This is where the canonical paper PDFs live.
- Both wg21.org and wg21.link point back to open-std.org originals. A common mistake is believing the papers live on wg21.org. They live on open-std.org and are linked.
- Known canonical URL (general knowledge, not stated in the three files, verify before linking): https://www.open-std.org/jtc1/sc22/wg21/

## wg21.link and paper discovery

- Every published paper number resolves at wg21.link. Format: https://wg21.link/p1234r0.
- wg21.link is run by a committee delegate and has shortened paper links for years with no committee objection.
- Paper numbering scheme (use for the chapter's "how to find papers" material):
  - `P` is a proposal.
  - `N` is an administrative document such as an agenda or minutes.
  - `D` is a pre-publication draft that does not resolve publicly. Cite the published `P` revision, never the `D` draft.
  - Revision suffixes run R0, R1, R2. Read the highest revision. When a paper returns, compare against the prior revision to read what changed.

## Participating without a paper

- Review papers on the wg21.org public review list. This is named as the lowest-commitment way to participate. (Mechanics above under wg21.org.)
- Scribe. Any participant may be asked to take minutes, in meetings and in telecons.
- Attend as a guest. Give the convener one week's notice, attend the Sunday 6pm orientation, and take full part in subgroup work and subgroup straw polls. Guests do everything except vote in plenary.
- NOT in sources, flag: "championing" another author's paper as a defined activity, and participating specifically in SG (study group) telecons. The sources support reviewing, scribing, and guest subgroup work, but not these two framings.
