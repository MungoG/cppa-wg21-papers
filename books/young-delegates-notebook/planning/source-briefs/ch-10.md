## What a Paper Is: Numbers, Format, Deadlines

Well sourced.

- [SD-7](https://isocpp.org/std/standing-documents/sd-7-mailing-procedures-and-how-to-write-papers) ("Mailing Procedures and How to Write Papers") tells you how to obtain a paper number, submit it, and format the document. It governs form, not evidence. `(P4 §4.1)`
- [SD-4](https://isocpp.org/std/standing-documents/sd-4-wg21-practices-and-procedures) ("WG21 Practices and Procedures") sets the surrounding procedure. `(P4 §4.1, P8 §8.1)`
- Paper numbers carry a revision suffix: P1234R0, then R1, R2. Read and cite the highest revision. `(P8 §8.2)`
- Prefixes: `P` is a proposal; `N` is an administrative document (agenda, minutes); `D` marks a pre-publication draft that does not resolve publicly. Cite the published `P` revision, never the `D` draft. `(P8 §8.2)`
- Every published number resolves at [wg21.link](https://wg21.link), written `https://wg21.link/p1234r0`. `(P8 §8.2)`
- Deadline: a paper must arrive before the pre-meeting mailing deadline. A late paper is not actionable and is not discussed in meeting time. `(P4 §4.1, P8 §8.2, §8.4)`
- Procedural floor, verbatim: "If a proposal does not have a paper, it does not exist." The entire floor is a paper, on time, with a prepared presenter. `(P4 §4.1)`
- Front-matter fields: title, document number, date, audience, reply-to, and intent. `(P4 §4.4)`
- Intent is declared in front matter as `intent: ask` (proposes, requests a poll, seeks adoption) or `intent: info` (places evidence in the record, asks for nothing). `(P4 §4.4)`
- Volume context: WG21 publishes 300 to 500 papers a year across pre- and post-meeting mailings. `(P8 §8.2)`
- Where papers live: the official archive at open-std.org is a flat chronological PDF list; the enhanced, searchable, subgroup-filtered mailing is at wg21.org and links back to the open-std.org originals. `(P8 §8.2, §8.7)`

## Formatting Tools and the Official Template

- `[not in sources]` None of mpark/wg21, Bikeshed, COWEL, LaTeX, or the isocpp.org template appears in the assigned sources. SD-7 is the only sourced format authority `(P4 §4.1)`; the tools below are general knowledge.
- mpark/wg21: Markdown plus Pandoc framework that renders committee-styled HTML and PDF; common default for new authors (high confidence).
- Bikeshed: preprocessor that generates HTML specs, by Tab Atkins-Bittner (high confidence).
- COWEL: newer markup tool aimed at C++ proposals, by Jan Schultke (low confidence on exact repo URL - verify).
- LaTeX: used for some papers and for the C++ working draft sources themselves.
- Official template: isocpp.org hosts submission guidance and a starter template (submit-a-proposal page, medium confidence - verify). mpark/wg21 and Bikeshed each ship their own starter template.

## Document Accessibility

- `[not in sources]` No accessibility guidance appears in the assigned sources. All general knowledge.
- WCAG basics for a paper: sufficient color contrast, a monospace font for code, a logical heading hierarchy, descriptive link text, alt text for figures, and no meaning encoded by color alone.
- Reference standard to cite: WCAG 2.1 AA.
- Writer action: do not claim WG21 mandates accessibility; no such requirement was found (low confidence any rule exists).

## Co-Authoring Papers

- Partially sourced.
- The `reply-to` front-matter field names the contact author. `(P4 §4.4)`
- Acknowledgments close the paper as a chain of custody, tracing every contribution to the person who made it. `(P4 §4.4)`

## Revision History and What Reviewers Look For

Well sourced.

- Revisions are explicit in the number: R0, R1, R2. When a paper returns, reviewers compare the new revision against the prior one to read what changed. `(P8 §8.2)`
- Common newcomer failure: raising a concern the author already resolved in a newer revision. `(P8 §8.2)`
- Review lenses (what a reviewer checks; run them on your own draft first): routing (which group), motivation stated, alternatives acknowledged, prior-art positioning, teachability from the paper alone, ABI impact, memory-safety implications, backward compatibility and migration, implementation evidence, and wording precision with edge cases. `(P4 §4.5; PRS narrow-lens table)`
- Principle: a lens that comes back empty is an objection you handed the room for free. `(P4 §4.5)`

## The Public Paper Review (before the meeting)

From PRS and `(P8 §8.6)`.

- A public, Boost-style review runs on WG21.org. The author requests a review, the paper is scheduled, and for exactly one week (Monday to Sunday) the community posts reviews on a public mailing list.
- Eligibility: the paper must already be published in the WG21 paper system. No pre-prints, no mailing-only drafts, because there is no published artifact to attach the summary to.
- An AI reviewer posts a narrow-lens analysis before the human window; after it closes, the AI validates each human review against the paper, and a credited summary PDF is attached to the paper with reviewers named.
- Review list: `paper-reviews@lists.wg21.org`. Readable without an account; to post, register with GitHub or Google OAuth, email hidden.
- Reciprocity culture: reviewing a paper earns the standing to request a review of your own.

## Proving It Works: Implementations and Compiler Explorer

- ISO does not require implementation before standardization. SD-4 states "there is no ISO requirement that something has to be implemented before it is standardized." `(P4 §4.1)`
- A strong paper carries one anyway. For a library: code that compiles, passes tests, carries benchmarks, and documents its API. For a language feature: a proof-of-concept compiler fork. `(P4 §4.2, §4.7)`
- Cost note (neutral): a complete library with tests, benchmarks, and docs is now days of work with AI assistance. The quality bar is deliberately low - it must compile, pass its tests, and demonstrate the design, not be production-grade. `(P4 §4.3)`
- `[not in sources]` Compiler Explorer (godbolt.org), by Matt Godbolt, runs code across many compilers in the browser and produces a shareable live link to embed as a working demo (high confidence).

## GitHub and Version Control

- Partially sourced.
- The review platform uses GitHub (or Google) OAuth to post. `(PRS; P8 §8.6)`
- Some committee working repositories were made private to stop decisions leaking live from meetings, so not all WG21 repos are public. `(P8 §8.5)`
- wg21.link (paper short links) is run by a committee delegate and has operated for years without committee objection. `(P8 §8.7)`
- `[not in sources]` Hosting a proposal's source on GitHub, using issues as a design-feedback tracker, and versioning revisions in git is general practice, not stated in the sources. mpark/wg21 and Bikeshed are themselves GitHub-hosted.

## Scheduling Your Presentation

- A proposal needs a qualified presenter in the room or it is not actionable. Verbatim: "A proposal without a qualified presenter in the room is not actionable either." `(P4 §4.1)`
- No paper means no existence; no presenter means no discussion. `(P4 §4.1; P8 §8.3)`
- `[not in sources]` The specific instruction to contact the chair in advance is not stated. General practice: email the relevant subgroup chair before the meeting to get on the agenda and confirm remote versus in-person presentation (medium confidence).
- Adjacent in-room constraints: every session is minuted and any participant may be asked to take minutes; live tweeting, blogging, photos, and video are barred, and recording is not permitted ([N4916](https://wg21.link/n4916)). `(P8 §8.3, §8.5)`

## The Other 80%

- `[not in sources]` The framing "design approval is only 20%, wording and ballot are the remaining 80%" is not stated in the sources; present it as the writer's own framing. The facts below are sourced.
- After design approval, wording is reviewed and goes into the working draft, which then proceeds to a national-body ballot. `(P8 §8.4)`
- Ballot stages: the DIS (Draft International Standard) is a three-month enquiry ballot needing a two-thirds majority of P-member national bodies. The FDIS (Final Draft International Standard) is an eight-week approval vote on editorial comments only. `(P8 §8.4)`
- Out-of-scope rule: a ballot comment requesting a feature absent from the balloted document is out of scope, and rehashing a settled matter is ruled out of harmony with the ISO Code of Conduct. `(P8 §8.4)`
- Chapter takeaway: design consensus is a milestone, not the finish line; wording review and the ballot phases hold most of the remaining work.
