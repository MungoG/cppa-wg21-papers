# Chapter 2 Fact Brief: Meet the Committee

Condensed source facts for the writer. Neutral and welcoming, no book prose. Verified facts come almost entirely from `wg21-bible` Part 1 and Part 8. The Delegate Essais description and the milieu document did not contain the INCITS, membership, orientation, or conference material this chapter expects, so several topics are flagged as unverified below.

## The Bureaucratic Nesting: ISO / IEC / JTC1 / SC22 / WG21

- ISO and IEC jointly own JTC1, the committee for information technology.
- JTC1 contains SC22, the subcommittee for programming languages.
- SC22 contains WG21, the working group for C++.
- Full path name: ISO/IEC JTC1/SC22/WG21.
- Authority to publish a standard sits at the top of the chain, not the bottom. A working group, per the ISO Directives, "operates by consensus, reports and gives recommendations, if any, to its parent committee through a Convenor." Working group decisions are recommendations. That is their full legal weight.
- A standard becomes real through National Body ballots at the SC22 level: Committee Draft (CD), Draft International Standard (DIS), Final Draft International Standard (FDIS). One country, one vote.
- Two rulebooks govern. The ISO/IEC Directives bind. SD-4 is WG21's own self-authored practices document, maintained by the convener, and is what participants actually read.
- Links: ISO/IEC Directives (`iso.org`, https://www.iso.org/directives-and-policies.html); SD-4 (https://isocpp.org/std/standing-documents/sd-4-wg21-practices-and-procedures).

## The Committee in Numbers

- Meetings are week-long, held three times a year, hybrid (onsite plus online).
- Recent meeting shape, from the convener's trip reports:
  - Brno, June 2026: 200 attendees, 55% in person, 28 nations, 25 new guests, 22 subgroups, 6 parallel tracks.
  - Sofia, June 2025: about 200 attendees, about 67% in person, 30 nations, 25 new guests, 23 subgroups, 7 parallel tracks.
- Subgroup pipeline: study groups (SG1 through SG23) incubate raw ideas; design groups set direction (EWG for the core language, LEWG for the library); wording groups settle specification text (CWG for the language, LWG for the library); plenary ratifies wording into the working draft.
- Structure count: 22 to 23 active subgroups, four permanent design and wording groups, roughly 13 active study groups, 6 to 7 tracks running in parallel during a meeting.
- The train model: since C++11, a new standard ships every three years (C++11, C++14, C++17, C++20, C++23, C++26, C++29). Cadence codified in P1000 (Sutter).
- Trip report links (Herb Sutter): Brno (https://herbsutter.com/2026/06/13/brno-trip-report/); Sofia (https://herbsutter.com/2025/06/21/trip-report-june-2025-iso-c-standards-meeting-sofia-bulgaria/).
- Population figures conflict across sources (see flags): roughly 350 active delegates, about 200 per meeting, and "roughly a hundred people in the room"; downstream users given as sixteen million or five million.

## National Bodies: What They Are

- A National Body (NB) is a country's standards organization participating in SC22. A P-member (participating member) NB casts one vote on each of the CD, DIS, and FDIS ballots.
- How to join: join your nation's standards body as a C++ participant. The US National Body is INCITS PL22.16.
- NB membership versus guest attendance: an NB member becomes an accredited representative (once listed in the global directory) and can vote in plenary. A guest takes full part in subgroup work but casts no plenary vote.
- Tone note: the sources frame NB powers through a reform lens (large unused authority). Keep the chapter neutral. Extract only what an NB is, the one-vote-per-country fact, and how to join.

## Membership Paths and Costs

Three paths put you in the room (SD-4 and Part 8). Pick by budget and by whether you need a vote.

1. National Body membership. Join your nation's body as a C++ participant. Cost varies by nation: some charge annual dues, some are free, the US body charges an annual fee. You can vote in plenary once your name reaches the global directory.
2. Boost Foundation alternate representative. Free. The Boost Foundation sponsors free alternate-representative status for C++ developers who cannot afford membership. The source states "The access is equivalent." Link: Boost Foundation C++ standardization (https://sites.google.com/boost.org/boost-foundation/c-standardization).
3. Guest attendance. Free. Give the convener one week's notice, attend the Sunday 6pm orientation, and you take full part in subgroup work. Guests do everything except vote in plenary polls.

Quick table (cost / plenary vote):

- National Body member: varies, US charges a fee / yes, once in the directory.
- Boost Foundation alternate: free / yes, when accredited.
- Guest: free / no.

Source guidance: if dues are the only barrier, the Boost path is free and access-equivalent.

## Registering as a Guest

- Give the convener one week's notice. (The sources say "one week's notice" and "give the convener notice." They do not specify that the channel is email. See flags.)
- Attend the Sunday 6pm orientation if you are new.
- After that you take full part in subgroup work: discussion, straw polls, revisions.
- Members register; guests give notice.
- Newcomer mistakes the sources call out: showing up with no notice and expecting a seat; assuming guest status carries a plenary vote.

## Guest Rules and Limits

- Guests do everything except vote in plenary polls.
- Only names in the ISO LiveLink global directory vote in plenary (SD-4).
- Guests can still shape discussion and subgroup straw polls.
- Unverified: the specific rule "one meeting as a guest, then you must join an NB" is not in the sources. See flags. What the sources do warn against is "attending for years without getting accredited," so a vote never counts when it matters.

## Voting and the Global Directory

- The chapter calls it the "ISO Global Directory." The sources name it the "ISO LiveLink global directory." Same thing; prefer the source term or note both.
- Accreditation means your name reaching that global directory. It is the prerequisite for a counting plenary vote.
- Plenary polls default to unanimous consent. On objection, a plenary poll becomes three-way: Favor, Against, Neutral. Only names in the directory vote.
- Subgroup straw polls use a five-point scale: Strongly Favor, Weakly Favor, Neutral, Weakly Against, Strongly Against. A proposal normally advances when those in favor outnumber those against by more than two to one (the 2:1 guideline, SD-4).
- Consensus is the ISO standard: "the absence of sustained opposition to substantial issues by any important part of the concerned interests." It is neither majority rule nor a minority veto.

## Roles You Can Play

- Note-taker. Every session, telecons included, must be minuted, and any participant may be asked to take minutes. A clear, low-barrier entry role. (verified)
- Reviewer. The lowest-commitment way to participate. Register with GitHub or Google (your email stays hidden), read a scheduled paper, and post what you found. The source says it "need not be exhaustive." Reviewing earns the standing to request a review of your own; giving precedes asking. This runs on the wg21.org paper-review system. (verified)
- Author. Submit a paper to the mailing. A proposal with no paper "does not exist," and papers must arrive before the pre-meeting mailing deadline. Paper-writing procedure is SD-7. (verified)
- Implementer. Implementers (compiler vendors) participate and give feedback, but the sources treat "implementer" as a participant type, not a documented newcomer on-ramp. (partially verified, see flags)
- Champion. Not defined as a role anywhere in the four sources. (unverified, see flags)

## You Don't Need Permission

- Concrete, verified support for "the barrier is lower than newcomers think": guest attendance is free and needs only one week's notice; the Boost alternate path is free and access-equivalent; reviewing a paper needs only a GitHub or Google login.
- The sources also use the explicit phrase that certain high-impact actions require "no one's permission," but they apply it to convener reforms and NB rights, which sit inside the reform framing. For a welcoming beginner chapter, anchor the low-barrier point in the concrete free and low-notice paths above rather than that framing.

## Resources to Hyperlink

Sites:

- isocpp.org: home of the standing documents (SD-3 through SD-10).
- open-std.org: the official mailing archive, a flat chronological list of PDFs.
- wg21.org: the enhanced, searchable mailing; volunteer-built and parallel, not official committee infrastructure.
- wg21.link: paper link shortener. Every paper number resolves as `https://wg21.link/pNNNNrN`.
- iso.org: ISO/IEC Directives and the ISO Code of Conduct.
- Boost Foundation C++ standardization page (link above).

Documents and numbers relevant to this chapter:

- SD-4: WG21 Practices and Procedures. Covers membership, the global directory, consensus, the 2:1 guideline, and guest rules.
- SD-7: mailing procedures and how to write papers.
- SD-3: study group organization. SD-5: meeting logistics and information.
- N4916: meeting guidelines that bar live tweeting, blogging, photos, video, and recording in sessions.
- P1000: the three-year train model cadence.
- P2274R0: committee-size comparison with WG14; also the source for "no requirement on implementation experience to adopt a proposal."
- INCITS PL22.16: the US National Body (no URL provided in sources).
